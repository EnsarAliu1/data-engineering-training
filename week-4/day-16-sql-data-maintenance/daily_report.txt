What I understood better today:
- Why soft deletes are standard practice in data engineering for preserving audit trails and preventing broken foreign keys.
- How SQL tri-state logic treats NULL values, requiring IS NULL for filtering and COALESCE for default output formatting.
- How anti-joins (LEFT JOIN + WHERE right_key IS NULL) reliably uncover missing operational records across related entities.

What was difficult:
- Constructing multi-table LEFT JOIN queries with multi-column join criteria (student_id AND assignment_id) to correctly isolate missing submissions without creating duplicate join rows.

One result I can explain and defend:
- Why updating an enrollment status to 'cancelled' (soft delete) is safer than executing DELETE FROM students. Deleting the student row triggers foreign key constraint errors or cascades deletion of historic attendance records, whereas a soft delete maintains accurate historical analytics while excluding the student from active rosters.
