What I understood better today:
- Why real-world databases rely on ALTER TABLE for zero-downtime schema evolution instead of dropping and recreating tables.
- How views simplify query logic for business reporting and protect downstream BI tools when underlying base tables change.
- How transactions act as a safety net during mass update/delete operations, enabling verification before committing.
- How indexing foreign key columns drastically improves join speeds by avoiding costly full table scans.

What was difficult:
- Structuring final_student_progress_view with multiple LEFT JOINs to avoid creating unintended Cartesian product duplicate rows when joining student submissions and attendance.

One result I can explain and defend:
- Why executing an UPDATE inside BEGIN TRANSACTION and verifying row count before ROLLBACK or COMMIT prevents catastrophic production data loss. In our test, rolling back an unconstrained update instantly restored original submission scores.
